Twelve questions in five parts, one part per chapter: three each on chapters 01 and 02, two each on chapters 03, 04, and 05. Closed book; every answer is worked, not stated — the derivations are the point. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the flagged chapters; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — what a proof is (chapter 01)

  1. Derive. The loop below must exit with p = 2^n:
   i := n;  p := 1;
   while i > 0 do
       p := p * 2;
       i := i - 1
   od

Supply the invariant in the sum-loop's shape (one arithmetic fact tying p to i and n, one range fact), then discharge all three obligations — initiation, consecution (naming the recurrence that is load-bearing), termination (name the variant, show it strictly decreases) — and state exactly what the while rule hands back at exit, and nothing more.

  1. Diagnose. Two incidents at a proof-assistant vendor, one week apart. (a) A 50,000-line tactic library is found to derive False on pathological goals; thousands of customers proved theorems with it. Must any theorem the kernel already accepted be re-proved? (b) A kernel bug — the guard checker admits a false fixpoint (the Coq 2018-2021 class) — is found and fixed in days. What must happen to the proofs that used the buggy rule, and what property of the stored artifact makes the repair cheap?
  1. Predict. A 4,000-line C component climbs the specification ladder: types, then defensive pre/post contracts, then a full functional spec with loop invariants, then machine-checked proof. Predict the line count at each rung, say where Dafny's binary search sits (three lines of logic, ten of contract), and give the one-sentence reason the top rung's multiplier is seL4's economics in miniature.

Answers

Part I

  1. I ≡ p = 2^(n−i) ∧ 0 ≤ i ≤ n. Initiation: i := n, p := 1 gives p = 1 = 2^0 = 2^(n−n), and 0 ≤ n ≤ n (n ≥ 0 is the caller's obligation). Consecution, under the guard i > 0: before the body p = 2^(n−i); after p := p * 2 it is 2^(n−i+1); after i := i − 1 the new exponent n − (i−1) = n−i+1 matches — the load-bearing recurrence is 2^(k+1) = 2·2^k, exponentiation's recursive definition, playing the role Σ's Σ_{k<i+1} = Σ_{k<i} + a[i] played in the sum loop. The range survives: i > 0 gives i−1 ≥ 0, and i−1 ≤ i ≤ n. Termination: variant v = i — the guard supplies v ≥ 1, each iteration decrements it by 1, ℕ is well-founded. Exit: the while rule hands back your own invariant plus the negated guard — I ∧ ¬(i > 0) = p = 2^n ∧ i = 0, and nothing else: facts absent from I cannot appear in the conclusion (ch01's rule; the weak-invariant lesson in reverse).
  1. (a) Nothing — the tactic layer is untrusted by design. LCF's move: thm is an abstract datatype with no public constructor; only the kernel's rules return one. Tactics propose terms, the kernel disposes — a tactic that mis-derives False on pathological goals wastes CPU there and nothing else; it cannot forge a theorem. Every theorem the kernel accepted was term-checked at Qed, so it stands. Trust sits in the checker, never the search. (b) The kernel is the TCB as a line count (Isabelle ~2,500, Coq ~30,000): a guard-checker escape is an implementation bug in a rule — the Coq 2018-2021 class. Proofs whose terms pass through the buggy rule must be re-checked, and the repair is cheap because the stored artifact is the proof term — the λ-term, Curry-Howard; the tactic script was already discarded at Qed. Fix the kernel, re-run the checker: coqchk re-typechecks a whole development without loading any tactic code. The 2000-2020 kernel-bug list is ~a handful precisely because the surface is that small — and once fixed, every old proof re-checks from its stored term (ch01's de Bruijn criterion: proofs as files, checkable independently of the prover).
  1. Types at ~0.1-0.3×: 400-1,200 lines. Defensive contracts at ~0.5-1×: 2,000-4,000. Full functional spec with loop invariants at 1-5×: 4,000-20,000. Machine-checked proof at 10-40×: 40,000-160,000 lines. Binary search's ten contract lines over three of logic ≈ 3.3× — the full-spec rung (1-5×); "the spec is 3× longer than the program" is normal, the chapter's own count. The miniature: 10-40× is seL4's raw 400k proof lines over ~9.3k C+asm ≈ 40:1 (the ~100:1 headline counts the grown tree with specs and generated artifacts) — the spec is the floor, the proof is the mass, and that ratio is why you prove the guidance component or the crypto primitive, not the whole app (ch03's sizing rule, previewed).

Part II

  1. (a) 10^4 × 2^24 × 2^9 = 10^4 × 2^33 ≈ 2^46.3 ≈ 8.6 × 10^13 states (10^4 ≈ 2^13.3). (b) × 32 B = 2^51.3 B ≈ 2.4 PiB — a ~46-boolean problem: between the chapter's 40-boolean 32 TiB "never" and 50-boolean 32 PiB, about 40,000× over the 64 GiB laptop and ~200× over the 12-16 TiB 8-socket ceiling. No machine on earth. (c) 8 B per state buys exactly one bit — 2.75 × 10^15 → 1.4 × 10^15 B, still ~50× past the ceiling; the 2 TiB box moves nothing. The constant is negotiable, the exponent is not: shaving bytes-per-state cannot help — the exponent eats any constant. What moves the frontier is removing variables: declare the 4 nodes symmetric (divides the space by ~4! ≈ 24, buys 4.6 bits), and bound the ballot counter to the values the property actually reads (24 bits → 3 bits buys 21 bits, a factor of two million). Both re-run the multiplication; neither touches the property. (d) 8.6 × 10^13 ÷ 10^6 states/s ≈ 8.6 × 10^7 s ≈ 2.7 years — patience kills the sweep even where RAM cannot (ch02's spine: states × bytes-per-state versus bytes-of-RAM, then time).
  1. (a) 5 variables → 2^5 = 32 rows. (b) The OR chain: x1's 1-edge is the T terminal and its 0-edge x2; …; x4's 0-edge is the all-false path, which still must test z — 4 tests + 1 z-test + 2 terminals = 7 nodes. (c) 32/7 ≈ 4.6×; at n = 20: 23 nodes against 2,097,152 rows (~91,000×) — the chapter's generalization, n + 3 nodes versus 2^(n+1) rows. (d) Interleaved, each (xi, yi) pair resolves before the next is tested, so every path that keeps "equal so far" reaches the same suffix sub-DAG — the partial state is one bit of summary, 3n + 2 nodes (8 at n = 2). Blocked, the x-tests run first and the residual after k x-tests is one of 2^k distinct functions of the y's — the DAG must remember the whole x-pattern: 10 nodes at n = 2, ~n·2^n at n bits. The same shape is the ripple-carry adder's linear-vs-exponential carries (interleave a0 b0 a1 b1…), and the multiplier is the honest limit — 2^Ω(n) under every order (Bryant 1991): when no order saves you, change the representation. Canonicity is why f ≡ g is one root compare — but only under a shared order.
  1. Error one: α ≈ 4.26 is a statement about random 3-SAT — the density at which near-certainly-satisfiable flips to near-certainly-unsat, with the hard instances packed at the edge; quoting it about a structured instance is memorizing the plot without the caption — the chapter's interview trap. Error two: the arithmetic (8,600/2,000 = 4.3) is right, the population is wrong — real instances sit off the curve: 8-queens runs α ≈ 18 and solves in milliseconds. CDCL eats structure — clustering, symmetry, intent: learned clauses and VSIDS's recency heap exploit recurring conflicts, which random instances never produce. The cheap observation next time: run it and watch the solver's own telemetry — restarts, clause counts, conflict statistics — an instance genuinely drifting toward its hard regime shows it there long before any timeout; α predicted nothing (ch02's easy-hard-easy curve and its caption).

Part III

  1. Certification is process evidence; a theorem is artifact evidence. DO-178C DAL A certifies how you worked: reviews, MC/DC structural coverage, traceability, independent verification — machine-checkable nowhere. seL4's theorem is a claim about what the artifact is: every behavior of the ~10k lines of C refines the abstract spec — re-checkable by anyone holding the proof, the l4v tree, and Isabelle (whose ~2,500-line kernel is the checker). INTEGRITY-178B is certified but not formally verified: no machine-checked theorem anywhere, and the advertising has let readers hear "proven." seL4's 2023 Common Criteria EAL6+ — the first general-purpose kernel at that level — is the honest composite: the theorem made the paperwork cheap (EAL6+ demands formal design artifacts; seL4 showed up with a formal implementation), while the certificate still adds what a proof cannot: independent eyes on the target and the delivery chain. Read "verified" in any datasheet as asking which rung (ch03's vendor-claim triage).
  1. The Linux kernel memory model (LKMM), written in the Cat language, machine-checked with herd7 (Alglave, Maranget, McKenney, Parri, Stern; formalized 2017; lives in tools/memory-model). Maintainers run litmus tests — "may this reordering happen?" — against a formally defined concurrency model, daily, in the ordinary flow of kernel development. What is machine-checked is the concurrency specification, not the C: the data-race-freedom arguments for core synchronization (RCU, the locking discipline) are made against it. So the camps both won: the microkernel-with-proof flies in drones — seL4's refinement theorem at the bottom, compartmentalization above; the monolith-with-knowledge made its concurrency spec a machine-checked artifact used as ordinary tooling — the field escaped the lab, each side holding a piece (ch03's Linux paragraph).

Part IV

  1. (a) The niche: None is the null bit pattern — and a valid &u32 is never null, so no live value collides with the discriminant and the tag is free: 8 bytes, no tag word. The same bet for Box, NonZero, and fn pointers. (b) The type system proves safety: no UB in safe code, given the library's invariants hold. Validity is the bit-pattern obligation unsafe code must maintain: a bool is 0x00 or 0x01 — the same eight bits are a valid u8 and an invalid bool, validity is type-relative; references must be non-null and aligned; char ≤ 0x10FFFF; uninitialized memory must not be read as typed data. (c) The read. Writing 0x02 into the field is legal bytes; the UB begins when it is read as a bool — and the layout optimizations (the niche itself, branch elimination on the discriminant) are sound only because validity is upheld: debug codegen happened not to exploit the invalid pattern, release did. (d) MaybeUninit is the honest type for uninitialized memory; the distinction is the RustBelt validity-versus-safety line (ch04's insiders).
  1. (a) async fn compiles to a state machine: each .await is a suspend point, and every local live across it becomes a field of the generated Future — buf, n, view, socket are all fields now. (b) view = &buf[..n] is a reference field pointing into a sibling field: the struct is self-referential. Moving the state machine relocates buf without rewriting view — the interior pointer dangles (module 01 ch04: a reference is an address into a frame; move the frame, the address is stale). (c) Tofte-Talpin stack order: a frame's values die with its frame, regions nest like frames. Suspension breaks it mid-function — a suspended future is a frame outliving its function, with live ranges crossing the suspend point into a compiler-generated heap struct whose region variables the programmer never names. (d) Pin<P> is a ghost proposition — "this value will not move again before drop," no runtime content, pure obligation carried in the types: !Unpin marks the types that depend on it, Box::pin is the safe derivation (the heap move before pinning is the last move), and Pin::new_unchecked is the admit hole. (e) The fragment is permission arithmetic on syntax — region variables, outlives edges, a least fixed point — with no formula language to state "this field points into that sibling field and the move discipline preserves it." Stating that needs x ↦ e, the points-to assertion: full separation logic, the undecidable part the fragment dropped to stay decidable. So the language ships the obligation as a type-level proposition instead (ch04's async section; module 19 ch03's Pin, returning with its proof-theory reading).

Part V

  1. Top to bottom: (1) mission software — unverified, by design, confined to its compartment; (2) flight-control and middleware apps — unproven; isolation is done by compartmentalization, each component holding only the capabilities it was granted — the capability distribution is what separates them. (3) the seL4 C kernel (~8,700-10k lines) — proven: functional correctness, every behavior refines the abstract spec, machine-checked in Isabelle. (4) the abstract specification — human-reviewed, ~10k lines: the gap that remains. (5) ~600 lines of hand-written assembly (entry/exit, context switch, fast paths) — unproven, audited by reading, kept small on purpose. (6) CompCert — the ~11 passes Clight→Asm are proven (each a forward simulation, UB-free source hypothesis, Beh(Asm) ⊆ Beh(source)), but its own trust base is nested: the Coq kernel (~30 kLOC, LCF-style — trusted, small by design); the C semantics model (trusted — the theorem is about this model, not ISO C verbatim); the Coq→OCaml extraction plus the OCaml compiler (unverified — the binary is tested software that emits proven software); the cpp + C parser front-end (unverified edge: a parsing bug puts you outside the theorem); the external assembler/linker and ~15 kLOC C runtime (unverified edges — the proof ends at Asm text). The edges are tested — Yang et al's Csmith/EMI harness is the discipline — not proven. (7) the COTS Arm board — assumed fault-free: Rowhammer, voltage/clock glitching, EM injection sit below every theorem above; the seL4 community's honest answer is glitch-detection hardware and redundancy, not more proof. (8) the delivery chain — verifiable, not verified: reproducible builds (bit-identical rebuilds, diffoscope) bridge the shipped bytes back to the source; SLSA-style attestation is signed claims, not a proof of the build. This instantiates module 19 chapter 05's "unsafe kernel with a safe membrane" at full-system scale: a thin unproven/assumed boundary wrapped around a proven core, the membrane made of a theorem plus capabilities.
  1. (a) 25 py ÷ 15 yr ≈ 1.7 person-years per year, total — and the theorem does not expire when you port the system built on it (DARPA HACMS reused it across platforms with no re-proof), so the per-consumer figure keeps falling. (b) $100M ÷ $200k = 500 person-years ≈ 20 seL4-scale proofs per incident — and the next CVE bills again, while the proof billed once. (c) CRUD endpoint: long-lived at best — 1/4, do not pay (the chapter's own scoring; the correct answer, not a failure). X25519 in a browser: adversaries hunt it, decade-plus lifetime, hundreds of millions of consumers, and failure silently leaks keys — 4/4, pay ("a crypto library or a compiler scores 4"). One-off migration script: 0/4 — delete it when it finishes. (d) Verification's price is up front and printed; the alternative's price is unbilled until the incident — the option that looks free is the one with the unbounded tail (ch05's boundary-and-economics close).
