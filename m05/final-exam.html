Module 05 final exam

every chapter · worked answers behind the fold

Twelve questions across all six chapters. Closed book; answers are worked, not stated — the explanations are the point. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the flagged chapters; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — boot & ART (chapters 01-02)

  1. (derive, cross-module 01 ch03) Zygote's preloaded framework footprint is F = 128 MB; 24 forked apps each dirty d = 2 MB of private COW copies. Compute total physical RAM for the naive JVM-per-app design and the zygote design. A change then makes a 16 MB preloaded region write-hot; per-app dirty memory rises to 14 MB. Recompute, name the broken invariant, and give the dumpsys meminfo column that shows the damage.
  1. (concept) AMS triggers every app fork through /dev/socket/zygote, a plain UNIX socket init created — not through Binder. Give two independent reasons: bootstrap ordering, and what fork() does to multi-threaded IPC state.
  1. (derive, cross-module 01 ch12) A class has two reference fields and one int. Compute per-object size and the footprint of one million instances with ART's compressed references on and off. State the decode formula, why ART has no 32 GB cliff where HotSpot does, and the header arithmetic behind 8 vs HotSpot's 12 bytes.
  1. (diagnosis) A news app throws OutOfMemoryError after 20 minutes of image-heavy scrolling. The profiler shows large-object space growing monotonically, allocation space flat, GC pauses sub-ms. What is leaking, why can compaction never reclaim it, what would an object-graph leak show instead, and what is the fix class?

Answers

Part I

  1. Naive: every app pays a private warm JVM: N × F = 24 × 128 = 3,072 MB. Zygote: fork() copies page tables, not pages — F + N·d = 128 + 48 = 176 MB, ~17× (ch01 §3; module 01 ch03). Write-hot region: 128 + 24 × 14 = 464 MB — the ratio collapses to ~6.6×. The broken invariant is the read-mostly discipline: apps must write few framework pages — ART allocates post-fork objects away from the preloaded heap for this reason (ch02). The damage shows as Private Dirty — the COW copies; shared pages stay dirty-0 (ch01).
  1. Ordering: AMS lives in system_server, zygote's first fork — the fork client cannot exist before zygote does; the trigger must predate the IPC universe it populates. Fork safety: binder state is process-global and multi-threaded (thread pool, mmap'd pool, handle tables), and fork() clones only the calling thread: forking mid-binder hands the child locks whose owners don't exist there (ch01 §4). Hence an init-created socket (mode 660 root:system), a single-threaded zygote in accept(), binder joining the child only after fork+specialize (ch03).
  1. On: 8-byte header + 2 × 4-byte refs + 4-byte int = 20 → 24 bytes (8-byte alignment). Off: 8 + 2 × 8 + 4 = 28 → 32 bytes; a million instances: 24 MB vs 32 MB (25% saved). ART reserves one 4 GB-aligned window and stores each reference's low 32 bits — decode window_base | stored32, no shift. HotSpot stores address >> 3, buying 32 GB of reach, hence its cliff where references widen to 8 bytes; ART's heapgrowthlimit caps heaps in the hundreds of MB, so the window never binds (ch02; module 01 ch12). Header: class 4 + lock 4 = 8 bytes — the lock word does quadruple duty (thin lock, fat lock, identity hash, GC forwarding); HotSpot pays 12.
  1. Large primitive arrays retained in large-object space — byte[] decode/serialization buffers. LOS admits only reference-free primitive arrays ≥ 12 KB (kLargeObjectThreshold = 3 × 4 KB); LOS objects are page-mapped and never moved or compacted (freed only by unmap), so retention is permanent page-granular cost no collection fixes. An object-graph leak would show allocation-space growth with LOS flat; the space split is the first-pass classifier (ch02; module 01 ch19). Fix: downsample, stream, and reuse the big buffers.

Part II

  1. (a) int32 length prefix (4, in UTF-16 units) + 2,000 × 2 = 4,000 UTF-16LE bytes + 16-bit NUL (2) = 4,006, padded → 4,008 bytes. (b) No: oneway transactions together may not exceed pool/2 = 520,192 B; 600 KB = 614,400 exceeds it even with the pool empty; the budget protects sync callers from oneway starvation. (c) Two: 3 × 350 KB = 1,075,200 B > BINDER_VM_SIZE = 1,040,384 B (1 MiB − 2 pages). The pool is per-process, the receiver's, shared by all in flight; the third fails though each call alone fits (ch03).
  1. The reply is itself a binder transaction whose receiver is the client: it must fit the client's own 1 MiB mmap'd pool; 1,350,213 > 1,040,384 → BR_FAILED_REPLY. The service's log marks its failed reply; the exception surfaces in the client's proxy — code the service's author never wrote (ch03). Free RAM is irrelevant: the limit is per-process buffer geometry. Fix: paginate the query, or ship bytes via SharedMemory — only a 24-byte BINDER_TYPE_FD rides the parcel; the payload never crosses Binder (ch03).
  1. Sixteenth-thread starvation. A synchronous call consumes its calling thread until the reply. All 15 pool threads (DEFAULT_MAX_BINDER_THREADS = 15) plus main are parked in transactNative, each waiting on a reply that needs the peer to call back into the app — which needs a 16th looped thread. The driver would spawn one (BR_SPAWN_LOOPER), but 15 is a hard cap: everyone waits on a delivery the kernel cannot make. Main parked in a binder ioctl past input dispatch is the ANR. The rule: no nested synchronous IPC — framework callbacks are oneway (ch03).
  1. P (935), then S (912), then Q (906). adj first, RSS as tie-breaker: importance dominates, so S's 90 MB dies before Q's 400 MB. R never enters the arithmetic: adj 200 is the perceptible band, below the 900 cached cliff — a foreground service anchors importance independently of RSS; the system won't trade audible playback for 500 MB (ch04). No heap, CPU, or age appears: lmkd executes the ranking AMS wrote. Newer refinement (Android 12+): per-process zram swap-in cost — a large cold process can out-rank a slightly higher-adj small one.
  1. (a) adj comes from lifecycle components (visible activity, foreground service, bound service), never CPU or threads: a bare Thread registers no importance, so onStop demoted it to the cached band (900–999, slot 906). (b) The budget path: (adj 906): cached #17 is AMS evicting the LRU cached process past DEFAULT_MAX_CACHED_PROCESSES = 32; the pressure path logs under the lowmemorykiller tag — Killing ... to free NkB rss — after a PSI trigger (70 ms some-stall/s). (c) Fix 1: a typed foreground service while syncing (adj ~200, below the cliff, freezer-exempt). Fix 2: design for death — WorkManager plus persisted progress. Relaunch is cheap: a zygote fork over resident COW-shared pages (ch01); state survives only if serialized (ch04).

Part III

  1. (a) SELinux MLS categories. Labels come from seapp_contexts (levelFrom=all): A runs s0:c23,c256, B's file carries s0:c7,c257; the category constraint requires matching sets, checked at the kernel's SELinux hook: avc: denied { read } ... permissive=0. (b) seccomp-BPF, verdict ERRNO → EPERM, at syscall entry, before the kernel touches the subsystem: userfaultfd is on the app deny list; filters are a one-way ratchet, inherited across fork, only intersectable with stricter. (c) The uid wall first: ptrace of a foreign uid dies EPERM in the owner check (SELinux denies it for untrusted_app besides). What survives all three: the binder surface, the map's soft part (ch05).
  1. Fails: 65,400 + 140 = 65,540 > 65,536; d8 reports Too many method references: 65540; max is 65536. The 180 call sites cost nothing because invoke-virtual {v0, v1}, method@BBBB carries a 16-bit index into the method_ids table; new call sites reuse existing entries, ids dedupe. New references consume the u16 address space (2^16 = 65,536), counting every referenced framework method, not just yours (ch06 §2). Ways out: R8 (deleting unreferenced methods deletes their ids) or multidex: classes.dex keeps startup classes, classes2.dex the rest (native in ART since 5.0).
  1. (a) A/B doubles the system partitions (_a/_b); the running slot is never written — update_engine streams the new image into the inactive slot, then flips the boot flag in misc (retry count 7; unhealthy slots fall back). Virtual A/B (Android 11+) drops the physical second slot: the diff applies into a copy-on-write snapshot on /data (ch01's fork trick at partition scale), merged by device-mapper once healthy. (b) The old slot: never written, so it boots; half-applied snapshot data is discarded — power loss mid-update is a non-event. (c) bsdiff works on uncompressed bytes; deflate streams avalanche under small input changes. Puffin "puffs" each stream to a canonical bit-level form, diffs those, re-deflates deterministically — the patched file is bit-identical, manifest hashes match. (d) The platform cert store on /system; dm-verity — a Merkle tree over its 4 KiB blocks, root hash signed in vbmeta via AVB, chained to the ROM — checks blocks as they are paged in; the store cannot rot at runtime (ch06 §4, ch01 §1). ROM → AVB → dm-verity → certs → APK → fork: hash the next thing before you trust it.
