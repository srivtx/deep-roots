Module 12 final exam

every chapter · worked answers behind the fold

Twelve questions, three per chapter. Closed book; every answer is worked, not stated — the derivations are the point. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the flagged chapters; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — the hierarchy (chapter 01)

  1. Predict. A kernel compiles to 80 registers per thread on an Ampere-class SM: 65,536 32-bit registers, 64 warp slots, 4 warp schedulers with 16 resident warps each. Derive resident warps, occupancy, and warps per scheduler, then decide whether a 400-cycle global load issued once per 16 instructions can be hidden — at this occupancy, and at any occupancy.
  1. Predict. A warp reads 32-bit shared memory at word strides 8, 12, and 15 (smem[lane * S]). For each: how many serialized passes, justified by the gcd rule? Which is fastest, and what is the general fix for a conflicted stride?
  1. Diagnose. A filter kernel branches per thread: if (v[tid] > 0) { two global loads + 300 math instructions } else { one load + 8 instructions }. Every warp splits ~50/50. The profile: 75% occupancy, 32 registers/thread, top stall "long scoreboard", issue efficiency ~50%. Trace the mechanism — what divergence does to issue efficiency and to the stall — and why 75% occupancy cannot buy either back.

Part II — coalescing & the roofline (chapter 02)

  1. Predict. struct V { float x, y, z; }; — a warp loads v[tid].x from an AoS array, base aligned. Derive sectors, transactions (128 B units), bytes touched, payload, efficiency; compare with SoA xs[tid]; and explain why the multiplier is 3x when a four-field struct gives 4x.
  1. Predict. A kernel does 8 FLOP per element and moves 16 B per element (three float reads, one write) on an A100 (19.5 TFLOP/s FP32, 2.0 TB/s HBM). Give arithmetic intensity, attainable FLOP/s, percent of peak, verdict. What minimum AI reaches 50% of peak? A teammate proposes halving the FLOP count — what does the roofline say that buys?
  1. Diagnose. A column-reduction kernel computes s[tid] += a[r*4096 + tid]: 4096 columns, a warp owns 32 of them, the loop walks rows. ncu reports sectors-per-request 32.0; the kernel runs ~8x slower than its row-sum sibling. Diagnose the access shape, derive the traffic, and give the one-sentence fix.

Part III — allocation & the real OOM (chapter 03)

  1. Derive. A 60 MiB toy device, 20 MiB segments. Alternating requests of 5 MiB + 1 B (→ 6 MiB blocks) and 3 MiB + 1 B (→ 4 MiB blocks) fill three segments, each [6][4][6][4] MiB; then every 4 MiB block is freed. An 8 MiB request arrives. (a) The rounded request and pool? (b) Derive, rule by rule, why it fails though free = 24 MiB ≥ 8 MiB. (c) Why does the same request succeed under expandable_segments:True?
  1. Diagnose. A training job on a 40 GiB card dies: "Tried to allocate 512.00 MiB; 3.1 GiB is reserved by PyTorch but unallocated." memory_summary: reserved 39.6 GiB, allocated 36.5 GiB, inactive splits 210, OOM events 5. The developer calls torch.cuda.empty_cache() — 0 MiB released — and retries twice. Identify the cause, explain why empty_cache() was guaranteed to release nothing, and give the fix ladder in cost order.
  1. Concept. A model holds 10,000 tensors of 64 KiB each. What do they cost through raw cudaMalloc, what through the caching allocator, and why does the caching allocator exist? Name the module 01 allocator it replays and the two taxes it removes.

Part IV — unified memory & the future (chapter 04)

  1. Concept (cross-module). Module 01 ch02's tower was managed by hardware (caches) plus one OS level (DRAM). The completed tower's manager column reads compiler → kernel author → hardware → driver → runtime/OS → application — each manager further from the silicon. Why must the manager get further away as levels slow down, and why is that why each faster level exists?
  1. Diagnose. A pipeline allocates 2 GiB of managed memory, the host initializes every page, then a kernel streams it once. The kernel phase takes 786 ms; the explicit-copy version totals ~90 ms. Nsight's unified-memory track shows ~1,024 faults. Derive the per-fault overhead, name the mechanism and its module 01 chapter, then give the one-line fix and the better fix.
  1. Concept (cross-module). Three things a CUDA-trained engineer gets wrong on Apple Silicon (module 07 ch06: Mach VM, Jetsam): why there is no "CUDA out of memory" and what limits memory instead; why a GPU-only benchmark drops 30-50% when the CPU streams simultaneously; and which machine — M4 Max or A100 — wins an 8 GiB host-born streaming workload, with numbers.
Answers

Part I

  1. 80 regs × 32 = 2,560 registers per warp; 65,536 / 2,560 = 25.6 → 25 resident warps (800 threads) = 39% of the 64-warp ceiling, ~6 per scheduler. Hiding needs warps ≥ latency / instructions between loads = 400 / 16 = 25 per scheduler. Six is nowhere near; even 100% occupancy supplies 16 — the ratio is unhideable at any occupancy. The fix is tiling or ILP, not more warps — ch01's ratio test, why ch02's tiling exists.
  1. Lane i hits bank (stride * i) mod 32; passes = gcd(stride, 32). Stride 8: gcd(8, 32) = 8 → 8 passes. Stride 12: gcd(12, 32) = 4 → 4 passes — stride 12 beats stride 8. Stride 15: gcd(15, 32) = 1 → all banks distinct → 1 pass, fastest. Odd strides are conflict-free; the fix for a conflicted stride is padding +1 (stride 33), not reordering (ch01).
  1. The 32 lanes share one program counter, so the warp executes both paths serially with the other side masked: ~311 instructions where a uniform branch would issue one — efficiency halves before any memory effect. The taken path's loads then stall the whole warp ~400 cycles: masked lanes cannot issue, and reconvergence waits at the merge point — the "long scoreboard" stall. Occupancy cannot buy it back: 75% is 48 resident warps, but each is equally divergent. Fix: make the branch warp-uniform (sort or partition the data by side) — the hardware refuses to predict control flow by design (ch01's SIMT trade).

Part II

  1. .x sits at byte 12·tid: addresses 0..372, span 376 B → 12 sectors, 3 transactions, 384 B touched, 128 B payload, 33%. SoA xs[tid] is 4·tid: 4 sectors, 1 transaction, 100% — a 3x traffic multiplier. The multiplier is span arithmetic, not struct-size / 4: a 12 B stride packs 2-3 threads per sector; the 16 B struct leaves two = 4x. Layout is a memory-hierarchy decision (ch02; module 01 ch06 at warp scale).
  1. AI = 8 FLOP / 16 B = 0.5 FLOP/byte — far left of the 9.75 ridge (19.5 / 2.0). Attainable = 0.5 × 2.0 TB/s = 1.0 TFLOP/s = 5.1% of peak: memory-bound, the FLOPs idle. 50% of peak needs AI = 9.75 / 2.0 = 4.875 FLOP/byte. Halving the FLOP count buys nothing — the ceiling is bandwidth / bytes; halving the bytes doubles speed (ch02's triage division).
  1. Each warp instruction reads 32 addresses 16,384 B apart: each thread's 4 B in its own 32 B sector — 32 sectors, 8 transactions, 1,024 B touched for 128 B payload = 12.5%, the stride table's worst row: 8x the row-sum's traffic, hence ~8x the time. Fix: stage coalesced — load contiguous row segments into shared memory (stride-1 warp loads), then accumulate columns from shared (ch02's tiling reflex).

Part III

  1. (a) 8 MiB — already a 2 MiB multiple, large pool. (b) Exact size class: no 8 MiB block — the lists hold 4 MiB blocks. Best fit ≥ 8 MiB: largest hole is 4 MiB. Coalesce: every hole is fenced by live 6 MiB blocks — adjacent frees would merge; none are. Cross segments: never — separate VA reservations. New segment: a fresh 20 MiB cudaMalloc against a 60/60-reserved device, refused at the driver boundary. Free 24 ≥ request 8; contiguous, policy-satisfiable space: 0 — no byte was consumed; the bytes were stranded by geometry and the no-move promise (ch03; module 01 ch05). (c) With expandable_segments:True, each freed 4 MiB block unmaps its 2 MiB pages — physical returns to the driver (mapped: 60 → 36 MiB) — and the 8 MiB request maps fresh pages into a fresh contiguous VA run: contiguity becomes a VA choice (ch03).
  1. Cause #1, fragmentation — the "3.1 GiB reserved but unallocated" line is the diagnosis; inactive splits 210 counts holes freed next to live blocks. empty_cache() releases only entirely free segments; at failure every segment holds a live block — which is what fenced the holes — so 0 MiB was structurally guaranteed. Fix ladder, cost order: steady tensor shapes (pad variable batches so block sizes repeat — free) → PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True (one env var) → smaller batch (throughput) → restart (last). Plan capacity by peak reserved, never the tensor sum (ch03).
  1. Raw cudaMalloc: the 2 MiB page is the smallest currency, so each 64 KiB tensor maps 2 MiB — 10,000 × 2 MiB = ~20 GiB for 625 MiB of data, a 32x tax — plus 10,000 slow, sometimes-synchronizing driver calls. The caching allocator rounds to a 512 B multiple (64 KiB is one already — no tax), packs 32 tensors per 2 MiB small-pool segment, and serves repeats from size-class free lists: ~625 MiB allocated, ~626 MiB reserved, ~313 cudaMallocs. It is module 01 ch19's segregated-fit replayed at device scale, deleting the page-granularity tax and the per-call cost (ch03).

Part IV

  1. A manager must decide, ahead of the data's arrival, what lives at its level — and its reaction time is bounded by its distance from the silicon. The compiler sees the whole program offline (registers); the kernel author stages tiles by hand (shared); hardware reacts in cycles (L2); the driver maps pages on faults in ~µs (HBM); the runtime/OS tiers cold pages to a CXL pool over ms; the application alone knows the semantics, so it owns RDMA placement. Each manager is too slow for the level above — so that level exists to absorb the latency the slower one cannot hide: a stack of progressively slower managers, each covering the one below (ch04's closing table — module 01 ch02's tower, fully grown).
  1. 2 GiB / 2 MiB = 1,024 pages, each faulting once as the kernel first touches it. Streaming the same bytes at ~25 GB/s costs 80 ms, so 786 − 80 ≈ 706 ms is pure overhead: ~690 µs per fault — trap, pin, 2 MiB DMA over PCIe, GMMU page-table fixup, warp restart, paid 1,024 times — ~10x streaming: the convenience tax, measured. The mechanism is module 01 ch03's demand paging (the disk became a PCIe link). One-line fix: cudaMemPrefetchAsync(p, bytes, device) after host init — the same bytes at streaming speed, ~90 ms total, matching the explicit copy with less code. Better: have the data born on the device (initialize with a kernel) — nothing ever crosses the bus (ch04's birthplace rule).
  1. (a) No VRAM wall: one LPDDR pool, one fabric — no second per-device memory to exhaust. The limit is the system-wide dirty footprint, enforced by Jetsam bands on Mach VM (module 07 ch06; module 06 ch02): one number to budget, no "clear the GPU cache" button to hunt for. (b) CPU and GPU share the one LPDDR fabric — M4 Max ~546 GB/s — budgeted as one pipe, never two: concurrent CPU streaming takes the fraction a GPU-only benchmark assumed it owned — the 30-50% drop. (c) The M4 Max wins: 8 GiB / 546 GB/s ≈ 14.7 ms; the A100 pays for the bridge the integrated design deleted — 8 GiB host-born crosses PCIe at ~25 GB/s (320 ms) plus 4 ms compute ≈ 324 ms. Lower peak bandwidth wins because the copy dominated (ch04).
