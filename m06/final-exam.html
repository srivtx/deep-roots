Module 06 final exam

every chapter · worked answers behind the fold

Twelve questions across all five chapters, plus the module 01/05 comparisons the module is framed against. Closed book; answers are worked, not stated — the derivations are the point. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the chapters your misses cluster in; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — the kernel and the kill policy (chapters 01-02)

  1. A Mach task owns exactly three things; a thread owns none of them. Name the three, name what a thread brings instead, and derive why task_for_pid is entitlement-gated from what a task port is worth.
  1. A phone maps 2 GB of dense framework text. For a 48-entry TLB, give the coverage at 4K and at 16K pages; for the mapping itself, give the page-table bytes at 4K and at 16K. State the factor both answers share, and the cost that buys it.
  1. An app holds 120 MB of resident dirty anonymous memory and 30 MB of touched clean file-backed pages. The compressor later folds 48 MB of the now-cold dirty pages into a 12 MB store. Give RSS, phys_footprint, and the RAM the kernel got back — then name which number the foreground band's limit checks.
  1. "Volatile purgeable memory is dirty by content and clean by promise." Explain the three-tier footprint economy behind that sentence, and derive why a 200 MB purgeable image cache can sit near zero footprint while a 200 MB decoded bitmap cannot.
  1. A user reports the app "crashes" while visible. The only artifact is a JetsamEvent-*.ips: bug_type 210, pageSize 16384. In the processes array, Music shows rpages 300,000 and no reason; your app shows rpages 260,000, states ["frontmost"], and reason "per-process-limit". Crash or kill? Who died, why did the bigger claim survive, and what do you fix first?

Part II — IPC and the launch path (chapters 03-04)

  1. "Checking a uid on every operation" and "holding a send right" both control access. What does ambient authority permit that a capability makes structurally impossible — use the confused deputy and the check-to-use window — and what does the capability not give you, which the audit token exists to supply?
  1. Your camera app hands a 12 MB photo to an XPC thumbnail service on every shot. A teammate proposes a plain TCP socket "for portability"; your Android counterpart says Binder would move it in one copy. For socket, Binder, and a page-aligned OOL descriptor with deallocate set: count the copies, name who frees, and name the first hard wall each hits.
  1. Sixty processes each fault, read-only, on the same three shared-cache data pages; the kernel fixes each at page-in into the cache's VM object. Count the fixed physical pages. Then process A writes a global on page 2, and process B writes a global on page 2 too. Recount — and state the invariant that makes your first count possible.
  1. A TestFlight build's first launch after every update takes 2.1 s; the second takes 380 ms. The team files "regression: dyld is slow," attaching a Mac profile captured with DYLD_PRINT_STATISTICS=1 (180 ms pre-main). Diagnose the user's number and the profiler's number — what was invalidated, and why did the instrument lie?

Part III — trust, and the two-platform view (chapter 05 + the comparisons)

  1. A stack overflow in an arm64e system process offers four moves: (a) a ret-to-libc chain through a corrupted saved LR; (b) replay of a leaked, correctly signed return address; (c) a jump to a legitimate function entry with controlled arguments; (d) blind-forged return addresses over 1,000 crash-loop restarts. Dead or alive, each, and why? Why does the same overflow still work in a third-party app's own code?
  1. A build script copies a codec dylib into the app bundle after the codesign phase. On macOS, with disable-library-validation, the app runs; on iOS the process is killed: 9 at first launch. What was missing from the signature, which two checks did iOS run that macOS was told to skip, and what is the pipeline fix?
  1. Android's Zygote and iOS's shared cache solve the same problem with opposite mechanisms. Which cost is amortized into a process and which into a file? Which design shares dirtied pages? And why does iOS's no-fork, kill-first model require the file answer?
Answers

Part I

  1. The task owns a vm map, an ipc space (the port-rights table), and threads; a thread brings only registers and a stack — the resource-container / execution-unit split (ch01). task_for_pid (trap 26) mints a task port: read/write over the victim's entire vm map. Ungated, that would turn every process's memory into ambient authority, so macOS gates it with taskgated and iOS with entitlements (ch01; the port machinery is ch03).
  1. TLB coverage: 48 × 4 KB = 192 KB; 48 × 16 KB = 768 KB. Page tables: at 4K an L3 table covers 2 MB, so 2 GB costs 1024 tables × 4 KB = 4 MB; at 16K it covers 32 MB, so 64 tables × 16 KB = 1 MB. Both improve by the same 4x: each entry covers 4x more VA at identical per-entry cost. The price is internal fragmentation — the unit of allocation, protection, and transfer quadruples; one touched byte in a sparse structure dirties 16 KB (ch01's bet on density).
  1. RSS = 102 MB (72 MB resident dirty + 30 MB clean; the folded pages left the page tables, and the 12 MB copy is unattributed). phys_footprint = 84 MB (72 MB internal + 12 MB internal_compressed; clean file-backed pages are excluded — droppable, re-faultable). RAM back: 36 MB — compression creates memory: 12 MB spent to park data that cost 48 MB resident. The band limit checks footprint: killing you frees the compressed copy — the debt an RSS-only tool cannot see (ch02).
  1. Three tiers (ch02; the ledger underneath is module 01 ch03): clean file-backed — not in footprint, dropped and re-faulted from the file; volatile purgeable — dirty by content, disposable by promise, not in footprint, held in 8 age groups and dropped oldest-first under pressure, the purge disclosed only when you re-mark; nonvolatile/dirty — billed as internal, compressed or killed with you. The 200 MB cache marked volatile carries a regeneration promise the kernel accepts as payment; the decoded bitmap is live anonymous data with no promise — billed, then compressed, then killed for.
  1. A kill, not a crash. bug_type 210 is the Jetsam report — no thread backtraces by design (crashes are 309, with stacks). The victim is the entry with reason: your app, at 260,000 × 16,384 ≈ 4.1 GB claimed while frontmost. per-process-limit means footprint crossed its own band's resident limit — a ledger check, not a comparison — so Music's larger rpages are irrelevant: charged to a different, bigger allowance, no global shortage required. Fix footprint itself: mapped assets instead of decoded copies, volatile purgeable caches — the dirty + compressed gauge showed this coming (ch02).

Part II

  1. Ambient authority re-derives permission from who the process is, per operation: anyone passing an identity check can steer a privileged process into acting for them (the confused deputy), and check and use are separated in time — a window to race. A send right cannot even name an un-granted port: names index a kernel-owned table, entries arrive only by delegation or inheritance, and every mach_msg validates against that table — possession is the permission, no check to fool, no window (ch03). What it cannot supply is the caller's identity: capabilities authenticate the channel, not who is on it. The kernel-stamped audit token (pid, pid-version, uids, code-signing word) names the caller for the handler's policy — checked per message, since the token follows the latest message (ch03; the signing word is ch05).
  1. Socket: two copies — into a kernel buffer, out again; the receiver frees its buffer. Binder: one copy into the receiver's pre-mapped ~1 MB pool — a 12 MB parcel dies as TransactionTooLargeException: the pool is the first wall (module 05 ch03). Mach OOL, page-aligned with deallocate: zero copies — the kernel moves the sender's pages into the receiver's map (the sender's mapping vanishes); the receiver owns them and must vm_deallocate or leaks them until exit. The ~2 GiB inline ceiling never applies: the payload rides out-of-line. Per shot, two 12 MB copies is a battery line too; large xpc_data already rides OOL (ch03).
  1. Three fixed pages. Each faulted page is fixed once — the kernel applies the chained fixups and inserts the page into the cache's VM object, which backs every mapper — and the uniform per-boot slide is the invariant that makes one fixed copy valid for all sixty: per-process slides would force a private fixed copy each — the 200 MB × N __DATA arithmetic again (ch04). After the two writes: five — A and B each take a private COW copy of page 2, the fixed original still serving the other fifty-eight; pages 1 and 3 stay single copies (ch04; the fault is module 01 ch03's).
  1. Neither number is a dyld regression. The 2.1 s is the first-launch regime: the update re-signed or replaced images, so the cached launch closure — keyed to an exact image set by UUID — was discarded; that launch paid the closure rebuild plus cold page-ins, the launch budget's one-time column (ch04). The 380 ms second launch is the steady state: closure validates, pages map warm. The 180 ms is an artifact: on macOS any DYLD_* environment variable disables closure use for that process, so the instrument measured the fallback doing full symbol resolution — a path users never take (ch04). If pre-main still hurts, audit +load and dylib count.

Part III

  1. (a) Dead — PACIBSP signed LR (B key, SP as context); the chain's AUTIBSP; RET authenticates a value nobody signed and fails with probability 1 − 2^−24. (b) Alive — a leaked signed return address is pre-forged: the (pointer, tag) pair is complete, and replay passes; leaks still convert to control flow. (c) Alive — ret guards the backward edge only; steering to legitimate entries with controlled arguments is the forward edge, CFI/BTI's rung. (d) Dead — keys re-roll at every exec, so 1,000 restarts are 1,000 independent 2^−24 attempts, no partial credit, each a loud logged kill; progress needs a same-process oracle — a signing gadget or a PACMAN-style leak. The same overflow works in a third-party app because app code is still plain arm64: PAC hardens the system code around your pages, not yours (ch05).
  1. Missing: the dylib was in no signature. Copied in after codesign ran, it appears in no _CodeSignature/CodeResources (sealed-resources) hashes, and no CodeDirectory covers its pages. On iOS, library validation requires every executable page to trace to a signature chain or trust-cache entry — the check disable-library-validation tells macOS to skip — and page-in validation (cs_validate_range, in the fault path) hashes the first faulted page, finds no matching slot, marks the process CS_INVALID, and SIGKILLs: unsigned code never materializes, so death lands at first use, not exec. Fix: sign after every bundle mutation; gate CI on codesign --verify --deep --strict (ch05).
  1. Zygote: amortized into a process. Android's apps fork from one warm parent and inherit its already-dirtied pages via COW — the sharing includes the dirt (module 05 ch01; module 01 ch03's fork-COW pointed at warm state). Shared cache: amortized into a file — pre-linked and pre-signed at OS build, mapped COW, data pages kernel-fixed and shared only while clean; a write is a private copy (ch04). The kill model forces the file answer: iOS denies third-party fork() and ranks every process for death by band (ch02) — a warm shared parent would be un-killable state every victim takes down with it, while the file's pages are clean, reclaimable for free, untouched by any death. Same pay-once economics, opposite halves of the COW machinery.
