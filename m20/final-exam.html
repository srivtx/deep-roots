Module 20 final exam

every chapter · worked answers behind the fold

Twelve questions in five parts: three on chapter 01 (the no-MMU world), three on chapter 02 (bare metal and the linker script), two on chapter 03 (interrupts and DMA), two on chapter 04 (the RTOS), two on chapter 05 (firmware security) — 3+3+2+2+2. Closed book; every answer is worked, not stated — the derivations are the point. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the flagged chapters; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — the no-MMU world (chapter 01)

  1. Derive. A Cortex-M4 with its 8 MPU regions must support, statically: all 512 KiB of flash (RO+X), a 16 KiB kernel-data block (RW+XN), two unprivileged tasks each with its own 2 KiB stack (RW+XN) plus a 32-byte guard region below it, one 2 KiB DMA-buffer region (RW), and the flash-controller registers privileged-only. Count the regions and derive what the design cannot afford. Compute the exact MPU_RASR word for (a) one task's stack at 0x20008000 and (b) its guard at 0x20007FE0 — and name the two standard escapes when a third task is added.
  1. Diagnose. Working F407 firmware is ported to an nRF52840 with the peripheral #defines copied unchanged. The product hard-faults "at random" — hours apart, always on a rare logging path — and the fault frame's stacked PC names a function that had already returned. Occasionally there is no fault at all, just a garbage sample. SCB->CFSR (0xE000ED28) reads BusFault with IMPRECISERR set. Derive the mechanism, both symptoms, and the fix that is not "add retries."
  1. Diagnose. A meter counts pulses on one flag and keeps time on another, in one read-to-clear status register:
   if (IRQ_STATUS & FLAG_PULSE) {
       if (IRQ_STATUS & FLAG_2MS_TICK) handle_tick();
       handle_pulse();
   }

Field data: the tick handler runs far under rate whenever pulse traffic is heavy, individual pulses vanish now and then — and the anomaly never reproduces under the debugger. Derive both losses, why single-stepping makes it vanish, and write the correct two lines.

Part II — bare metal: the linker script is the memory model (chapter 02)

  1. Predict the number (RAM budget from the .map). The map excerpts: .data VMA 0x20000000 size 0x1C0 (LMA in flash); .ramfunc VMA next, size 0x120 (>RAM AT> FLASH); .bss (NOLOAD) size 0x5B40; ._user_heap_stack reserves _Min_Heap_Size = 0x200 and _Min_Stack_Size = 0x400; the theorem is ASSERT(_end + 0x600 <= _estack) with _estack = 0x20008000, RAM = 32 KiB. (a) RAM committed by the theorem. (b) Headroom. (c) The team adds static uint8_t rx_ring[6144]; and static uint8_t dma_buf[2048]; — does the build link, and if not, by how much? (d) Worst-case interrupt nesting is 2-deep, each handler's own frame is 40 B, the deepest thread-mode call tree is 880 B: audit the 0x400 reservation and give the sizing verdict.
  1. Predict. A flash-burn routine is marked __attribute__((section(".ramfunc"))); the script places .ramfunc > RAM AT> FLASH and emits _siramfunc/_sramfunc/ _eramfunc — but the startup code is the stock vendor file, whose copy loop moves only .data. What is at the routine's address the first time it is called, what does the call do (a) on real silicon and (b) under QEMU, and what is the two-line fix?
  1. Diagnose. To reclaim flash the team builds with -ffunction-sections -Wl,--gc-sections. The image shrinks by 9 KiB and the board hard-faults before reaching main; the map shows no .isr_vector. Derive why the linker's reachability analysis drops a table that hardware reads, what word[0] of the new image now is and what the core does with it, and the one-word fix.

Part III — interrupts and DMA (chapter 03)

  1. Derive. The 16-line SPSC ring is ported with three changes, reviewed as "the same queue, generalized": put is now called from two ISRs (UART and ADC), both __DMB()s are deleted ("the M4 is in-order"), and the indices become signed int ("cleaner comparisons"). Derive the ring's safety conditions from first principles — one writer per index above all — then map each change onto the condition it violates and the field symptom it produces, and compute when the signed version first corrupts if the producer side runs at 1,000 puts/s.
  1. Predict the number. Audio out on a 96 MHz M4: 48 kHz stereo 16-bit, DMA ping-pong with 512-byte half-buffers, the TC ISR re-arming the finished half. The system's longest interrupt-disabled window is 2,200 cycles; a higher-urgency flash write can run 3 ms. (a) Bytes/s. (b) The TC period and its re-arm deadline. (c) The disabled window in µs — is it the problem? (d) The flash write: glitch or not, in bytes? (e) Bytes to ride out 5 ms of jitter; and the cost of a 200 ms safety margin (ping-pong, ×2) as a fraction of a 128 KiB part.

Part IV — the RTOS (chapter 04)

  1. Predict the number (the heap is a config flag). FreeRTOS: five application tasks at usStackDepth 128 plus the idle task (configMINIMAL_STACK_SIZE 128), one queue of 16 items × 4 bytes (queue object 76 B), TCBs ~90 B, 8 B allocation header per heap_4 block. (a) Derive the bytes and the minimum configTOTAL_HEAP_SIZE. (b) Where do those bytes live at link time? (c) A reviewer says "128 is plenty — each task uses only 64 bytes." What does the third argument of xTaskCreate actually say, and what happens to a task given 64 with a 300 B call chain? (d) What does xTaskCreateStatic buy that heap_4 cannot?
  1. Predict. Three periodic tasks: sensor 4 ms / WCET 1 ms, control 12 ms / 3 ms, telemetry 24 ms / 9 ms. (a) Compute U and the Liu-Layland bound for n = 3 — verdict? (b) The periods are harmonic: what changes, and what is the honest verdict? (c) The fusion update measures 13 ms: recompute U, and construct which deadline is missed first, by how much. (d) In all three cases, what did the kernel itself guarantee?

Part V — firmware security (chapter 05)

  1. Walk the timeline (power loss through the A/B state machine). The v7→v8 update writes the new image to bank B, then performs two marker operations: PREPARE (write gen6 -> B into the spare slot) and COMMIT (erase the old gen5 -> A marker); markers are CRC'd {generation, bank} records and the bootloader picks the highest valid generation. (a) Walk all four power-cut points of the correct order: what is on disk, what the bootloader picks, and whether rollback is still legal. (b) A reviewer proposes erasing the old marker first ("the spare slot is wasted"). Walk the reordered sequence and identify every state from which no valid marker survives. (c) Where does the anti-rollback eFuse burn in this sequence, and why exactly there?
  1. Predict the number. A boot ROM's signature check is a 200-cycle sequence; the go/no-go branch occupies one cycle. A $300 glitch rig lands one randomly placed skipped instruction per boot; a reset-boot cycle costs 5 s. (a) Check-once: the per-boot win probability and the expected time to own the device. (b) Check-twice, the second check 80 cycles later and re-derived: why one glitch cannot win, and the expected time. (c) Which rung of the cost ladder is this, what does the countermeasure cost, and why must the second check re-derive its boolean rather than copy it?
Answers

Part I

  1. Count: flash 1 + kernel 1 + two task stacks + two guards + DMA 1 + flash-controller 1 = 8 — the entire budget, zero spare. The design can afford exactly two guarded tasks; a third task, a second non-cacheable buffer, or an .rodata write-protect each costs something already spent — 8-16 regions is a scarce protection state, not a page table. (a) 2 KiB = 2^11 so SIZE = 10; XN (bit 28, 0x10000000) | AP = 3 (0x03000000) | (10 << 1) | ENABLE = 0x13000015; 0x20008000 is 2 KiB-aligned, valid. (b) 32 B = 2^5 so SIZE = 4, AP = 0, enabled: (4 << 1) | 1 = 0x00000009; 0x20007FE0 is 32-byte-aligned and abuts the stack base, so the first stray down-growing write faults — MemManage, faulting PC stacked in the frame, ch01's precise method-3 overflow check. Escapes for a third task: alias two tasks into one shared RW region (isolation halved), or reprogram per-task regions at every context switch — FreeRTOS-MPU's arithmetic: 3 fixed globals + 3-4 regions rewritten per switch, tens of cycles folded into the ~150-cycle switch (ch04).
  1. The #defines are F407 wiring; on the nRF part that range is a reserved hole — the map is per-family, not per-ARM (only 0xE0000000, the ARM system region, is standard). Loads/stores into a hole raise a BusFault, and the write buffer makes it imprecise: IMPRECISERR means the fault surfaces some instructions after the offending access, so the stacked PC names the function that already returned — fault forensics one call too late. The no-fault/garbage runs are the per-SOC escape: some decode gaps return the last bus value or garbage instead of faulting. "Hours apart" because only a rare logging path touches the register. Fix: per-family vendor headers, never copied addresses — "which chip, which revision?" — and keep the log-and-reset policy for the residual.
  1. The register is read-to-clear: every read consumes every pending flag. Read #1 (the pulse test) already clears a simultaneously pending tick flag, so the inner test can never see it — handle_tick loses every tick coinciding with a pulse, hence the under-rate that tracks pulse traffic. Read #2 (the tick test) clears whatever latched between the two reads — including a fresh pulse bit that no branch ever tests: the vanished pulses. Two reads, one handled flag each at best — the side effect lives in the read half. The debugger: the watch window's refresh is a read, consuming flags while you watch, so the interleaving you are observing never occurs free-running — ch01's "flags evaporate while you watch / the bug disappears when I single-step" signature. Fix — read once into a local, test bits on the local:
   uint32_t st = IRQ_STATUS;              /* the ONE consuming read */
   if (st & FLAG_2MS_TICK) handle_tick();
   if (st & FLAG_PULSE)    handle_pulse();

Part II

  1. Static sum = 0x1C0 + 0x120 + 0x5B40 = 0x5E20, so _end = 0x20005E20. (a) Committed = _end + 0x600 = 0x20006420 = 25,632 B. (b) Headroom = 0x20008000 − 0x20006420 = 0x1BE0 = 7,136 B. (c) 6,144 + 2,048 = 8,192 B > 7,136: the build refuses to link, short by 1,056 B — the ASSERT is a theorem over the closed world of static allocations, and the binary is unproducible: oversubscription cannot ship (module 19's soundness in five seconds). The arrays shrink or RAM grows; "it fit yesterday" is not a state you may occupy. (d) Per nesting level: 32 B auto-stacked frame + 40 B handler = 72 B; × 2 levels = 144 B; + 880 B deepest thread tree = 1,024 B — exactly the 0x400 reservation: zero margin. Verdict: the reservation is a claim and the nesting arithmetic is its audit — run the 0xDEADBEEF pattern fill under worst case, scan for the high-water mark, and write measured + 25% (1,280 → 0x500) into _Min_Stack_Size.
  1. Nothing copies the routine: at first call its VMA in SRAM holds power-up garbage (real silicon) or QEMU's zero-fill — the bytes exist only at the LMA in flash. (a) On silicon the call lands on random bytes: undefined instructions or garbage execution, HardFault or silent corruption. (b) Under QEMU the zeroed SRAM decodes 0x0000 as a valid lsls-class instruction and the PC slides through a NOP sled until it hits real code — "works in the emulator, dies in the field," the same zero-fill masking that hides a deleted .bss loop. Fix — the copy loop .data already uses, duplicated for the ramfunc symbols:
   uint32_t *src = &_siramfunc, *dst = &_sramfunc;
   while (dst < &_eramfunc) *dst++ = *src++;   /* LMA -> VMA, again */

One routine, two homes, reconciled at rung 7 — ch01's ramfunc trick is ch02's LMA/VMA mechanism with a section name of your choosing.

  1. No instruction references the vector table — hardware, "an agent outside the ELF," is its only reader, and gc-sections garbage-collects unreferenced input sections. With the table gone, the image begins with .text: word[0] — which fetch hardware loads into MSP at reset — is now instruction bits, a garbage stack pointer; word[1] "PC" is not a handler entry, so the core jumps into mid-code on a corrupt stack and HardFaults before the first C instruction. The fix is one word: KEEP(*(.isr_vector)) in the script — the annotation that an agent outside the ELF reads this; the C-array version's __attribute__((used)) is the same annotation. The rule generalizes: anything whose only reader is not the linker's reachability graph (hardware tables, DMA descriptors nothing calls) is one build away from vanishing.

Part III

  1. The safety conditions, each with the failure it prevents: (1) single writer per index — head written only by the producer side, tail only by the consumer; (2) data published before the index (put's DMB — release); (3) index read before the data in get (acquire pairing); (4) wrap-safe unsigned arithmetic — h - t == N, never a signed comparison; (5) the index is one aligned 32-bit store, v6-M's only atomicity promise. The port: two producers = two writers of head — both ISRs read the same h, both write buf[h & 63], one byte overwrites the other and an increment is lost: corruption exactly when the ISRs coincide, and no barrier fixes a design error (one ISR must own head, or the index needs ldrex/strex). DMB removal breaks (2) and (3): on an in-order M4 the barrier mostly stops the compiler, which may sink the data store past head = h + 1 (consumer reads a fresh index over stale bytes — over-read) and float the tail store above the slot load (producer over-writes mid-read — the checksum-divergence flavor of lab 2). Signed indices break (4): at 1,000 puts/s the 2^31 wrap arrives after 2^31/10^3 ≈ 2,147,484 s ≈ 24.9 days of uptime, then h - t goes negative, full and empty both lie, and the ring floods or wedges — the "perfect for a month in the field" signature.
  1. (a) 48,000 × 2 ch × 2 B = 192,000 B/s. (b) 512 B ÷ 192,000 B/s = 2.67 ms per half — with the other half already armed, the TC ISR's re-arm deadline is one half-period, ≈ 2.67 ms. (c) 2,200 + 12 cycles = 2,212 ÷ 96 MHz = 23 µs = 0.9% of the deadline — the disabled window is not the problem; the thing that outranks you is. (d) 3 ms > 2.67 ms: the pipeline runs dry by (3 − 2.67) ms × 192,000 B/s ≈ 64 B = 16 stereo frames of silence — the audible click. (e) 5 ms × 192,000 = 960 B pre-buffered; a 200 ms margin, ping-pong ×2, is 200 ms × 192,000 × 2 = 76,800 B ≈ 59% of a 128 KiB part — "audio-capable" is a RAM-size class, not a CPU-speed class. Verdict: correctness here is function + timing; fix the priority structure (flash write below the TC ISR, or chunk the write), don't buy RAM to hide a scheduling bug.

Part IV

  1. (a) Per task: TCB 90 + 8 header = 98; stack 128 words = 512 + 8 = 520; ≈ 618 B. Six tasks (five + idle) = 3,708; queue = 64 storage + 76 object + 8 = 148 → 3,856 B, so configTOTAL_HEAP_SIZE = 4 KiB with ~6% headroom. (b) The heap is ucHeap[configTOTAL_ HEAP_SIZE] — a static array in .bss: zero file bytes, counted by ch02's ASSERT theorem, and OOM strikes at boot (create every task and queue in main before vTaskStartScheduler) where you can see it. The "heap" is a config flag, not a subsystem. (c) The third argument is usStackDepth in StackType_t words — 128 words = 512 B, not 128 B. Given 64, the task owns 256 B; a 300 B call chain overflows downward, smashing the 0xA5 fill — method 1's canary fires at the next context switch, naming the task in the overflow hook one switch late (the MPU guard faults the write itself). (d) xTaskCreateStatic puts stacks and TCBs in your .bss with sizes the linker proves: heap_1's "allocation is bring-up only" discipline enforced by the linker script instead of convention — fragmentation becomes a link error, i.e. impossible.
  1. (a) U = 1/4 + 3/12 + 9/24 = 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.375 = 0.875; Liu-Layland for n = 3 is 3(2^(1/3) − 1) = 0.780. 0.875 > 0.780: the sufficient test fails — the bound cannot prove this set. (b) The periods divide (4 | 12 | 24): harmonic, and the bound relaxes to 1.0 — 0.875 is schedulable with margin 0.125. Ship it; the bound is sufficient, not necessary, and harmonic sets are exactly where the tight answer is U ≤ 1. (c) U = 0.25 + 0.25 + 13/24 = 1.042 > 1.0 — demand exceeds supply, a miss is certain. RMA priority = 1/period: sensor > control > telemetry. Timeline from t = 0: the 1 ms sensor job every 4 ms and the 3 ms control job every 12 ms consume 12 of the first 24 ms, leaving telemetry 12 of its 13 — its first deadline (t = 24) is missed by 5 ms (it finishes at t = 29, after the sensor and control jobs released at 24 are served), and job 2 starts 5 ms behind with U > 1: the backlog grows monotonically while sensor and control still meet theirs. (d) Nothing: the kernel sells bounded mechanisms — the ~150-cycle switch, O(1) CLZ dispatch, priority-ordered wake — never deadlines. "Real-time" is offline arithmetic against measured WCETs; the datasheet's word is marketing.

Part V

  1. (a) CUT 1 (mid-write of v8 into B): A = signed v7, B = torn, markers unchanged (gen5 -> A) → boots A, update restarts — harmless by construction: the pointer never moves until the target is complete and signed. CUT 2 (torn PREPARE): B = complete signed v8; the spare slot's marker is torn (CRC fails, ignored), the old gen5 -> A is valid → boots A; the update re-runs step 2 only. CUT 3 (mid-erase of the old marker): both markers valid — or erase done, only gen6 -> B → boots B, complete and signed. No single flash write is atomic; the tie-break rule — highest valid generation — resolves every intermediate state to a valid image: the flip is atomic by policy, not by hardware. CUT 4 (B booted, unconfirmed): B runs in trial mode — N boots to confirm, else the bootloader re-points at A. Rollback is legal at every cut: the fuse has not burned and bank A still holds v7. (b) Erase-old-first: a cut during the erase leaves the only marker half-erased → zero valid markers; and if the following write-new is itself cut, the sole slot holds a torn marker → zero again. The reorder opens a two-cut window with no valid marker — the one unrecoverable state, a brick. The correct order's invariant is "at least one valid marker at every instant" — module 14 ch03's journal ordering: the write order is a design rule, not a style choice. (c) The eFuse burns to c = 8 only after the app confirms a successful boot of v8 — never at download or first boot: burn early and a lemon update makes the trial's legal rollback impossible, because the one-way counter then refuses the only good image — the boot-looping fleet.
  1. (a) One glitch must land on the single branch cycle: p = 1/200 = 0.5% per boot → ~200 boots × 5 s ≈ 17 minutes of resetting (the chapter's simulation: 67/10,000 = 0.67%, same order). (b) Both branch cycles — say 100 and 180 — must be skipped in the same boot: (1/200)² = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ → ~40,000 boots × 5 s ≈ 2.3 days of uninterrupted glitching, and the 80-cycle spacing means one wide glitch cannot span both checks. The attack economics flipped for the price of a second verify. (c) Rung 1 — the $300 ChipWhisperer; the countermeasure is code: check-twice plus fault-detecting control flow (a token walked through registers and checked after the jump — module 02's CFI shrunk to the branch scale), tens of bytes, ~zero runtime. The second check must re-derive its boolean: a copied ok2 lives in the register one glitch corrupts — both branches then test the same corrupted value and both "pass"; only an independent re-verification is unsatisfiable by a single corrupted result. And note the target: the check, not the crypto — fault injection buys a skipped branch, never a forgery.
