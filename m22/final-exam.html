Module 22 final exam

every chapter · worked answers behind the fold

Twelve questions in five parts — three on the tensor (chapter 01), three on the autograd tape (chapter 02), and two each on sharding (chapter 03), inference serving (chapter 04), and collectives (chapter 05). Closed book; every answer is worked, not stated — the derivations are the point, and the arithmetic is the engineering. Scoring: 11+ = you own this module; 8-10 = solid, revisit the flagged chapters; <8 = the chapter quizzes are the study path.

Part I — the tensor: strides, layouts, dtypes (chapter 01)

  1. Diagnose. A 40-block conv stack trains at a peak of ~7.6 GiB of activations. After a refactor the peak doubles to ~15.3 GiB — no layer was added, no error fired. Every block runs x.transpose(1, 3).reshape(B, C*H*W) on activations [32, 512, 56, 56] fp32 that arrive NCHW-contiguous, and each block's input stays live for backward. (a) The stride tuples before and after the transpose, and the merge-condition check that forces a copy. (b) The hidden-copy bytes per block and for the whole stack. (c) The one-line assertion that catches it, and the fix.
  1. Derive. A conv layer's inner channel loop sums C = 192 channels at one spatial offset; activations are fp16, lines are 64 B, spatial is 14×14. (a) NCHW: the channel step in bytes, lines fetched, bytes fetched, useful bytes, line occupancy. (b) NHWC: the same numbers. (c) The reuse factor, and why it is 32 here when the chapter's fp32 case produced 16. (d) In what sense is channels_last a metadata-only relayout — what changes, and what never does?
  1. Predict. A serving region reboots after a power incident: each node mmaps a 70 GB fp16 safetensors checkpoint from local NVMe (7 GB/s). (a) Cold-start time per node, and why the second load on the same box takes ~1 s. (b) The same checkpoint served in 4-bit. (c) A monitoring feature keeps a 4 MB .narrow() view of one 70 GB mmap'd tensor alive indefinitely — what is pinned, and what is the one-line fix?

  1. Predict. A 16,384-GPU synchronous data-parallel run: each GPU independently has a 0.05% chance per step of a 2×-slow step (thermal, congestion). (a) Expected stragglers per step. (b) P(no straggler) — and what the fleet's step time therefore is. (c) At a nominal 2,000 tok/s/GPU, what the tail costs in tokens/day, and why buying more GPUs cannot fix it. (d) The mitigation ladder, one line per rung, and the one insight all rungs share.
Answers

Part I

  1. (a) Contiguous [32, 512, 56, 56] fp32 has strides (1,605,632, 3,136, 56, 1). transpose(1, 3) swaps shape and stride entries: shape [32, 56, 56, 512], strides (1,605,632, 1, 56, 3,136) — zero bytes moved, a view. reshape(32, 1,605,632) must merge dims 1–3, permitted only when stride[i] == stride[i+1]·size[i+1]: 1 == 3,136·56 = 175,616 is false, and 56 == 3,136·512 is false — row-major order is unreachable in place, so reshape silently calls contiguous(): the hidden copy. (b) 32 × 512 × 56 × 56 × 4 B = 205,120,896 B ≈ 196 MiB per block; × 40 blocks = ≈7.6 GiB. The original is still live (the preceding op's tape entry saved it — chapter 02's blow-up rule), so the copy is additive: peak 7.6 → 15.3 GiB, the "2× with no code change" signature of an upstream layout change. (c) Assert x.is_contiguous() at the block entry (or compare untyped_storage().data_ptr() across the reshape). Fix: materialize the layout conversion once per step at the pipeline boundary — one 196 MiB copy instead of 40 — or consume the NCHW tensor directly and transpose the weight.
  1. (a) NCHW: the channel step is H·W = 196 elements = 392 B; each channel's 2-B fp16 lands in its own 64-B line: 192 lines, 12,288 B fetched, 384 B useful — 3.1% occupancy, 32× waste, and each line's other 62 B belong to another channel's row 392 B away. (b) NHWC: the channel step is 1 element; 192 consecutive fp16 = 384 B = 6 lines, 100% occupancy. (c) Reuse = 12,288 / 384 = 32× — line size ÷ element size (64/2) once C ≥ 32; the chapter's fp32 case is 64/4 = 16. The factor is line arithmetic, not a property of the layout. (d) channels_last rewrites the stride tuple (the C stride becomes 1) — a stride decision, not a buffer decision; is_contiguous(memory_format=…) is a predicate over strides. permute gives the channels-last view free when the bytes already sit in that order; .to(memory_format=…) moves bytes only because the buffer was materialized in the other order. Tensor Cores demand NHWC-shaped fragments, which is why cuDNN's fast fp16/bf16 kernels are channels-last — module 12 ch02's coalescing rule on the C axis.
  1. (a) safetensors is an 8-byte header length + a JSON map + one flat buffer; loading is mmap + parse, so tensors return as views over the mapping — page-cache-backed (module 14 ch03). Cold: 70 GB ÷ 7 GB/s = 10 s per node; the second load hits resident pages — page-table setup, ~1 s. On a fleet reboot that 10:1 × model count is the user-visible outage, which is why it is an incident. (b) 4-bit: 35 GB ÷ 7 GB/s = 5 s — quantization halves cold start along with residence. (c) A view is (offset, strides) over the whole refcounted StorageImpl, so the 4 MB .narrow() pins all 70 GB of the mapping (the offset-storage trick: one small slice holds the entire buffer). Fix: .clone() the 4 MB when the view must outlive the batch.

Part II

  1. (a) fp32 master 4 + fp16 compute copy 2 + fp16 grads 2 + fp32 m, v 8 = 16 B/param × 7e9 = 112 GB > 80 GB — plain mixed-precision Adam does not fit at all. (The master exists because fp16's 10-bit mantissa rounds an update lr·g ≈ 2⁻¹⁷ against a weight of 1.0 away to nothing — the fp16 copy is compute-only.) (b) 8-bit optimizer: m, v in int8 with 2,048-element block scales = 2 B/param of state: 2 + 2 + 2 + 4 = 10 B/param = 70 GB. (c) ~10 GB minus ~1 GB of context and workspaces ≈ 9 GiB; at ~4.8 MiB/token that buys ≈ 1,900 tokens: micro-batch 1×1024 (4.9 GiB) fits, 2×1024 (9.8 GiB) OOMs — the batch staircase. Conclusion: on one GPU the resident state, not the activations, is the wall — chapter 03's sharding imperative is this multiplication, not fashion, and the paged optimizer (state in CUDA unified memory, faulting to CPU DRAM) is the rung between 70 and 112 GB.
  1. (a) The three attention rows total 1,152 MB = 47% of the layer; flash attention tiles Q·Kᵀ into SRAM and computes the softmax online (running max and denominator), never writing the S×S matrices to HBM — so per-layer = 2,464 − 1,152 = 1,312 MB; × 12 layers ≈ 15.4 GiB. (b) + 3.07 GiB head ≈ 18.5 GiB (from ~32 GiB — a ~42% cut, the biggest software lever before any checkpointing). (c) 18.5/1.32 ≈ 14× the fp16 weight bytes (down from ~22×); against fp32 parameter bytes, ~7×. (d) Those rows are [8, 16, 1024, 1024] — heads × seq², quadratic in sequence length; every other row is [T, 2048]-shaped, linear in tokens. Long context multiplies exactly the rows flash attention deletes.
  1. Mechanism: leaf .grad buffers are persistent state and every backward accumulates into them with += — that is the feature when the accumulation window is one step, the bug when the window never closes. After k optimizer steps .grad holds the sum over 8k micro-batches ≈ 8k·ḡ, so the k-th update is applied with k× the intended magnitude — the effective learning rate grows linearly, which is the linearly climbing grad-norm and the divergence after a healthy first step. No retain_graph is needed: each micro-batch builds its own tape and .backward() frees it after one sweep; the retain error only fires when one graph is backwarded twice. Fix: optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True) at the top of each window — it drops the buffers (lazy reallocation on next use: no memset), releases the bytes, and leaves None meaning "never computed," so unused parameters are skipped and wiring bugs print as None, not a stale 0.

Part III

  1. (a) State = 16 × 70e9 = 1.12 TB; plain DP replicates all of it: 1,120 GB per GPU — 14× over an 80 GB part. (b) At N = 8: ZeRO-1 shards the 12 B/param optimizer block: 140 + 140 + 840/8 = 385 GB. ZeRO-2 also shards gradients: 140 + (140 + 840)/8 = 262.5 GB. ZeRO-3 shards everything: 1,120/8 = 140 GB — still over the 45 GB budget (80 − 30 − 5). (c) N ≥ 1,120/45 = 24.9, so N = 32: 35 GB of state + 30 + 5 = 70 GB, 10 GB of margin (N = 16 gives 70 GB of state + 35 = 105 — no). (d) ZeRO-3's parameter AllGathers fire in forward and backward, putting communication at ~1.5× plain DP — bandwidth traded for memory, a good trade only on a fast fabric. And at N = 8 even ZeRO-3 fails, so the missing axis is TP: its traffic is two latency-tight AllReduces per layer — NVLink-shaped — which is exactly why TP stays intra-node while PP's sparse, latency-tolerant activation send/recvs span nodes.
  1. (a) Base 4-bit: 70e9 × 0.5 B = 35 GB (dequantized on the fly into fp16 compute — read-mostly, never written). Adapters: 0.4% × 70e9 = 280M params — fp16 weights 0.56 + fp16 grads 0.56 + fp32 master 1.12 + fp32 m, v 2.24 = 4.48 GB of trainable state. Total ≈ 39.5 GB, leaving ~7 GB for activations and runtime on the 48 GB card. (b) Full fine-tuning is 16 B/param = 1,120 GB → ~28× compression — obtained by refusing to store what you are not updating. (c) The paged optimizer keeps m, v in CUDA unified memory, so capacity pressure faults optimizer pages to CPU DRAM — an implicit, fault-driven ZeRO-Offload. (d) Freeze what you don't train, page what you rarely touch: the frozen 4-bit base is malloc's read-mostly generation at terabyte scale.

Part IV

  1. (a) 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2 B = 327,680 B = 320 KiB/token (the 2 is K and V; only the 8 KV heads pay, not the 64 query heads — GQA). (b) Pool = 80 − 35 − 5 = 40 GB; block = 16 × 320 KiB = 5 MiB → 8,192 blocks. (c) A 4k context = 1.25 GiB = 256 blocks → 32 sequences; 8k = 2.5 GiB → 16 — the pool, not the FLOPs, sizes the product. (d) The prompt is 64 blocks, shared once and refcounted like a PyObject; each request then needs 256 − 64 = 192 private blocks: (8,192 − 64)/192 = 42 concurrent (+31%), and the 42 × 64 × 5 MiB = 13.1 GB of identical prefix KV collapses to 320 MiB — ~98% saved (a 100-request flood would duplicate 31.25 GiB). (e) fp8 halves per-token bytes: 2.5 MiB blocks, 16,384 of them, 64 sequences at 4k — the pool doubles. For contrast, MHA at 64 KV heads would pay 2.5 MiB/token = 10 GB per 4k sequence, 4 sequences in the whole pool: GQA's 8× cut is why the node works.
  1. (a) AI = 2·7e9·B / (14e9 + B·1e9). B = 1: 14/15 = 0.93 FLOP/byte; B = 64: 896/78 = 11.5 FLOP/byte — both far left of the ridge 312/2.0 = 156 FLOP/B: deep on the memory-bound slope, a memory stream, not a computation. (b) B = 1: 2e12/15e9 = 133 tok/s; B = 64: 64 × 2e12/78e9 = 1,641 tok/s. (c) The 14 GB of weights amortize across the batch (14/B GB per token — 64× less at B = 64) but each sequence's 1 GB of KV is private and never amortizes: as B → ∞ the cost per token approaches the KV read alone, ceiling ≈ 2e12/1e9 ≈ 2,000 tok/s aggregate — the KV floor no batch buys past. (d) 4-bit: B = 1 → AI = 14/4.5 = 3.11, 444 tok/s (3.3×); B = 64 → 896/67.5 = 13.3, 1,896 tok/s (~1.15×). Batching slides the point ~12× right by amortizing weight bytes; quantization shrinks the bytes themselves — 3× for the B = 1 latency product, where weights are the whole denominator, and marginal at large B. Hence W4A16: weights take group-128 4-bit scales; activations stay 16-bit because they hold the ~0.1% outlier channels at ~100× magnitude (LLM.int8's finding).

Part V

  1. (a) Reduce-scatter: at each of the N−1 steps, every link simultaneously carries one D/N chunk; chunk c leaves its home, hops rightward folding in partial sums, and after N−1 steps sits fully reduced at one owner — per link (N−1)·D/N bytes. All-gather: N−1 more steps circulate the reduced shards until everyone holds all N — another (N−1)·D/N. Total per link: 2(N−1)/N × D. (b) → 2D as N grows: each GPU's egress converges to "send every byte twice" — adding GPUs adds FLOPs and memory, not per-GPU communication; the ring meets the Chan–Gropp–Thakur lower bound of (N−1)/N·D per phase, hence bandwidth-optimal. (c) 2×7/8 × 14 GB = 24.5 GB per link; time = 24.5 GB ÷ 400 GB/s ≈ 61 ms, plus 14 hops × ~1 µs = 14 µs — negligible: bandwidth-bound, as gradient-sized tensors always are. (d) The price is latency: 2(N−1) serialized hops — a flat 25,000-GPU ring is 50,000 hops, hopeless for small tensors. Trees give O(log N) latency but the naive root bottlenecks at O(N·D) per link (recursive-halving/double-tree recovers both); the crossover is ≈ N·L·B, and NCCL's tuner picks rings above it, trees below, then builds hierarchy: intra-node NVLink 8-rings joined by an inter-node ring of nodes — 25,000 ranks collapse to ~3,100 ring members.
  1. (a) 16,384 × 5×10⁻⁴ = 8.2 stragglers per step, expected. (b) P(none) = 0.9995^16,384 ≈ e^(−16,384×0.0005) = e^−8.2 ≈ 2.7×10⁻⁴ — 99.97% of steps contain at least one 2× straggler. The all-reduce is a barrier in disguise: the step time is the max of 16,384 draws, and the max of that many samples is the far tail, every step — a per-GPU p99.9 of 2× median becomes the fleet's median, so the fleet runs at ≈ 2× a single GPU's median step. (c) Nominal: 16,384 × 2,000 ≈ 3.3×10⁷ tok/s ≈ 2.8×10¹² tokens/day; running at the tail roughly halves it to ≈ 1.4×10¹²/day. More GPUs make it worse, not better: E[stragglers] = N·p scales with N and P(none) → 0 faster — you cannot FLOP your way out of a max-of-N statistic. (d) Ladder, weakest assumption first: redundancy/speculative replay (recompute the straggler's work on a spare — idle duplicate FLOPs); ~1-step staleness (async-Adam proceeds with the stale gradient and reconciles); local SGD, N-step sync (k micro-steps on local weights then average — communication ÷ k, graceful degradation); 1-bit SGDM (quantized stale gradients buffered over 16–32 steps still converge, with momentum compensation). The shared insight: synchrony is a convention the math tolerates breaking — SGD's own noise already dominates bounded asynchrony.
